## 4. Distinction and relationship between the two laboratories

This section is a **working responsibility distinction for source reconciliation**, not a recovered canonical authority contract.

| Dimension | Physics Lab | Technology Lab |
|---|---|---|
| Primary question | What physical principles and quantitative models are supported by theory and observation? | What useful technologies can be designed, built, measured, compared and improved using established and testable principles? |
| Main subject | Physical systems, laws, parameters, boundary conditions, constitutive models and measurable predictions | Materials, devices, components, processes, architectures, products and integrated systems |
| Mathematics | Mathematical physics, derivations, dimensional/type analysis, analytical solutions, numerical verification, inference and uncertainty | Applied mathematics, design optimisation, computational modelling, control, system identification, reliability and multidisciplinary simulation |
| Main evidence | Independent derivations, benchmark problems, calibrated measurements, controls, uncertainty and replication | Requirements-linked model runs, prototype data, process characterisation, component/system tests, manufacturability and reliability evidence |
| Typical outputs | Qualified physical model or prediction within stated scope; measured constants; verified numerical benchmark; documented contradiction or unresolved result | Technology candidates, designs, models, prototype revisions, performance envelopes, process windows and integration evidence |
| Acceptance focus | Correctness within physical/model assumptions; reproducibility; uncertainty; agreement or disagreement with physical observations | Fitness against frozen engineering requirements; safety; reliability; performance; manufacturability; lifecycle and integration |
| Boundary | Does not certify a device simply because an underlying equation is consistent | Does not establish a new law of physics merely because a prototype or simulation works |

### 4.1 Shared mathematical foundation

Both labs need a shared, provenance-preserving mathematical capability, with domain-specific methods and assumptions. Potential method families include:
- symbolic algebra, exact arithmetic and theorem/formal verification where suitable;
- calculus, differential equations, PDEs, variational methods and mathematical physics;
- linear algebra, eigenproblems, modal analysis, numerical linear algebra and matrix/tensor methods;
- numerical analysis, discretisation, solver stability, conditioning, convergence and error estimation;
- continuum mechanics, elasticity, fluid dynamics, thermodynamics and transport;
- electromagnetics, optics, photonics and circuit theory;
- solid-state physics, semiconductor/device equations and materials structure-property models;
- quantum mechanics and quantum-device models where relevant;
- control theory, system identification, estimation, signal processing and robotics;
- optimisation, sensitivity analysis, design of experiments and multi-objective/Pareto analysis;
- probability, statistics, Bayesian inference, uncertainty quantification, reliability and risk;
- computational geometry, algorithms, information theory and machine learning for engineering/scientific tasks.

These are candidate coverage domains, not a new mathematics registry or an instruction to activate every discipline. Each method must state its assumptions, valid domain, units, solver/version, numerical limitations, uncertainty and evidence maturity.

### 4.2 Recommended interaction sequence

1. Technology Lab frames a technology objective and alternatives.
2. Physics Lab supplies or independently audits relevant physical principles, benchmark models and physical predictions where needed.
3. The mathematics/engineering toolchain performs dimensional, analytical, numerical and sensitivity checks.
4. Technology Lab develops comparable candidate models and prototypes against the same requirements.
5. Physical tests use the appropriate calibrated measurement chain; raw data and uncertainty are preserved.
6. Independent challenge and domain qualification assess the evidence.
7. A human-authorised engineering decision selects, combines, retains, defers or rejects the candidate.
8. Qualified outcomes and unresolved issues are preserved through the existing provenance/knowledge system.

A technology model must not become a Physics Lab result by being simulated. A physical measurement must not become a qualified technology merely because it is accurate. The purpose, conditions and acceptance criteria differ.

## 7. Lab capability layers — working proposal only

A useful future assessment may evaluate the following capability layers. Do not treat this as a final physical facility design or approved software architecture.

1. **Technology Observatory:** literature, standards, patents/prior art, supplier and manufacturing intelligence, technology maturity, external benchmark tracking and uncertainty.
2. **Mathematical and Computational Workbench:** symbolic/numerical mathematics, notebooks, validated solvers, simulation, optimisation, inverse problems, uncertainty and reproducible model runs.
3. **Materials and Device Engineering:** materials characterization, device/component models, electronics, photonics, sensors, mechanical/thermal integration and fabrication-process research.
4. **Prototype and Integration:** modular test articles, interface definition, hardware/software integration, control systems, digital twins and design-for-manufacture.
5. **Technology Test and Qualification:** requirements-linked tests, equipment identity/calibration, raw data, analysis, uncertainty, reliability, fault conditions, safety and independent review.
6. **Knowledge and Reuse:** preserve models, designs, rejected candidates, process windows, lessons and revision-specific test evidence through existing canonical DingoOS provenance/knowledge facilities.

The Technology Lab's role would be to generate and qualify technology-development evidence. Existing DingoOS subsystems should retain their own authority: mathematics checking, workflow execution, challenge, evidence custody, qualification and human approval are not to be reimplemented as a competing lab-specific framework.

## 8. Verification and laboratory evidence ladder

Keep these states distinct:
- concept / research question;
- sourced external claim;
- mathematical derivation;
- computational model or simulation;
- code/solver verification;
- solution verification / convergence;
- prototype demonstration;
- calibrated physical measurement;
- model validation for a stated use;
- independent replication;
- domain qualification;
- human-authorised engineering decision;
- controlled release/deployment.

A successful mathematical derivation does not demonstrate a material or device. A successful simulation does not prove that the model represents the physical prototype. A prototype pass does not establish reliability, manufacturing yield or safety outside its test envelope. These limitations must remain visible in both labs' reporting.
